About this tool

CSS Gradient Generator Online

Build linear, radial and conic gradients by dragging the stops directly on the artwork instead of guessing at numbers in a form. Every stop carries its own alpha, the midpoint between any two stops is adjustable, and the result comes out as CSS, SVG, a Tailwind value or JSON — or downloads as a PNG or SVG at any size you need.

Why this gradient generator is different

Most gradient sites give you two color inputs, an angle slider and a copy button. That is fine until you need a third stop in a specific place, a transparent end for an image overlay, or a ramp that doesn't go gray in the middle. Those are the ordinary cases this tool is built around.


Editing happens on the artwork

Stops are handles on the gradient axis itself. Drag the axis end to rotate, drag a stop to move it, drag the center or radius of a radial. The numbers follow your hand rather than the other way round.


Alpha is a first-class value

Every stop has its own opacity, shown against a checkerboard so you can see it, and preserved through the CSS, SVG and PNG exports. Fade-to-transparent image overlays take about four seconds.


OKLab interpolation, with a fallback

Blend in sRGB like every other tool, or in OKLab so complementary colors stop turning muddy halfway. Output the modern one-line syntax, or a sampled version that works in browsers that don't support it yet.


Midpoint control per pair

Push the halfway color toward either end and the tool writes it as a CSS color interpolation hint — a real part of the spec that almost no generator exposes, and the cleanest way to bias a two-stop ramp.


Banding taken seriously

Wide, low-contrast ramps band visibly on 8-bit displays. The grain option dithers the gradient — in the preview, in the PNG, and as a CSS snippet you can keep — which is the standard fix and rarely offered.


It checks the text on top

A gradient is usually a background. This one samples along the ramp and reports the worst-case contrast for black and for white text, so a headline doesn't disappear at one end of the artwork.

How does the gradient generator work?

A CSS gradient is a list of colors with positions along a line. Everything else — angle, shape, repetition — is how that line is laid over the box.

01
Pick a type

Linear runs along a straight line, radial spreads from a point, conic sweeps around one. Repeating tiles the whole ramp end to end.

02
Place the stops

Drag them on the canvas or the rail, click the rail to add one, and set each color's hex and alpha in the picker.

03
Shape the blend

Choose sRGB or OKLab interpolation, nudge the midpoint between any pair, and add grain if the ramp is wide enough to band.

04
Copy or download

Take the CSS, SVG, Tailwind value or JSON, or export a PNG or SVG at a preset or custom size.

Worked example — a three-stop linear gradient with a transparent end

The overlay pattern used on almost every hero image: solid at the bottom, gone by the middle. Note the third value in each color — that is the alpha.

.hero__scrim {
	background-image: linear-gradient(
		to top,
		rgb(18 16 12 / 0.85) 0%,
		38%,
		rgb(18 16 12 / 0.35) 55%,
		rgb(18 16 12 / 0) 100%
	);
}

The bare 38% on its own line is a color interpolation hint: it moves the halfway point of the first blend without adding a stop. That is the midpoint control in this tool, written the way the spec intends.

Gradients — frequently asked questions

The problems that come up once a gradient leaves the design file.

Why does my gradient turn gray in the middle?

Because it is being blended in sRGB. Halfway between two roughly opposite hues, the channel averages cancel out and you get a desaturated middle. Switch interpolation to OKLab and the blend keeps its chroma, taking a path around the hue circle instead of straight through the middle of it.

Why does fading to transparent go through gray?

Because transparent is shorthand for transparent black, so the color is travelling toward black as it fades. Fade to the same color at zero alpha instead — this tool always writes the full color with an alpha of 0, which is why its fades stay clean.

What causes those visible stripes across a large gradient?

Banding. An 8-bit channel has 256 steps, and a wide, low-contrast ramp may need more distinct values than the distance provides, so it quantises into bands. The fixes are more contrast, a shorter ramp, or dithering — which is what the grain option does, both in the CSS and in the exported PNG.

Should I use a CSS gradient or an exported image?

CSS whenever you can — it costs no bytes, scales to any size and stays sharp on any display. Export an image when the gradient has to live somewhere CSS cannot reach: an email background, an OG or social card, a slide deck, or a favicon.

How do angles work in CSS gradients?

Zero degrees points to the top and the angle increases clockwise, so 90deg goes to the right and 180deg to the bottom. This trips people up because many other graphics systems measure from the right and go anticlockwise. The gradient line is also sized so the box corners land exactly on 0% and 100%.

Can I put a gradient on text or a border?

Yes to both, and the “Try it on” panel above shows each. Text needs background-clip: text with a transparent color; borders need two backgrounds with different background-clip values, since border-color cannot take a gradient. Keep an eye on contrast for text — thin strokes lose the light end of a ramp first.
